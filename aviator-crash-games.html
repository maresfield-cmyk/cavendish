How the Aviator Game Works

Spribe, a Georgian-founded game studio, released Aviator in 2019, and the mechanic takes about one round to learn. A plane takes off, a multiplier climbs from 1.00x, and you cash out before the plane crashes. Leave it too late, and your stake is gone. Unlike slot games, there are no reels or paylines involved.

The game is provably fair. Before any round starts, the server generates a secret seed and publishes its cryptographic hash, effectively committing to that seed without revealing it. Client seeds are also used in determining the round result, with Aviator's fairness information showing the seeds from the first three players to place a bet. After the round, the information is revealed so the result can be independently checked.

That helps settle the question the rest of this page keeps returning to. The final crash point cannot be known in advance from the available information because it depends on a hidden server seed and the client-seed inputs used for the round. That means an app, Telegram group, or other outside service cannot reliably predict the next crash point.

Every round stands alone. The history panel shows what has already happened, but previous results do not determine the next one. Spribe Aviator also gives you two bet panels for running two stakes in a round, auto cashout that closes a bet at a multiplier you set beforehand, and a live chat alongside the game.

Why Every Aviator Predictor Is a Scam

SCAM WARNING

The crash point is not determined until bets are placed. Any "tool" or "prediction program" running before that moment would have nothing to read, and anything after is already too late. There is no way a prediction is possible. This is why no Aviator predictor works. It's just a mathematical impossibility.

You contribute a client seed, but the server seed stays secret until the round is over. Even if you somehow knew your own input before, it would still be useless.

Proof videos look convincing because they are edited. At 97% RTP, the chance of a round reaching any given multiplier is roughly 0.97 divided by that multiplier, so about 48% of rounds reach 2x. A channel calling 2x every round is right nearly half the time. And posts only the half it got right!

Aviator Scam Signs

✕A person/group/program asks for your betting account login. Nothing that reads public game data needs your password, and handing it over gives a stranger your balance.
✕You are told to download an Aviator predictor APK from outside official app stores, where nobody checks what is inside the file before it reaches your phone.
✕Someone is charging a subscription for "Aviator signals". 
✕Promo videos show wins only, often recorded in demo mode, which uses fake money anyway rather than a real balance.
✕Nobody selling one has ever posted a losing session. Not a bad week, not one clip of the tool being wrong. If there was a way the game could be hacked, Spribe would stop offering it!

Downloading dodgy software can compromise your data and security. Check Point Research analysed a campaign in which fake Aviator Predictor downloads delivered a Rust-based clipboard hijacker, malware that quietly swapped copied crypto wallet addresses for the attacker's own.

Aviator RTP: What 97% Actually Means

Aviator RTP (return-to-player) from Spribe is 97%, meaning a 3% house edge measured across millions of rounds, and it makes no promise at all about a run of ten. The game is high volatility on top of that, so short sessions swing hard in both directions.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does the Aviator predictor work?
No. The crash point is not generated until the first three players place their bets, so there is nothing to read in advance and nothing any tool can reach. Predictor apps sell the appearance of accuracy by posting only their correct calls and hiding the rest.
Can Aviator be hacked?
No. Aviator runs on a provably fair system where the server seed is hashed and published before each round, then revealed afterwards so anyone can check the result. Anything sold as a hack is either a subscription for random guesses or a way to get software onto your phone.
Where can I play Aviator in Ghana?
At any operator holding a Gaming Commission of Ghana licence and offering the game. The sites covered here are SportyBet, Betway and Betano, all three licensed and confirmed to offer Aviator. Check the operator against the Commission register before you deposit.

Is there an Aviator app?
No. Aviator runs inside an operator's own app or mobile site rather than as a standalone download, so anything calling itself the Aviator app is an operator app, a demo, or something you should not install. Install steps sit on our betting apps page.

What is the best time to play Aviator?

Is Aviator rigged?
No. The provably fair system means each result is fixed before any bet is placed and can be checked afterwards by anyone who wants to. Spribe's random number generator is certified by independent testing laboratories.
